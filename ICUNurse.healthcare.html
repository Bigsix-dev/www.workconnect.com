ICU Nurse

Full Job Description

An ICU (Intensive Care Unit) Nurse provides specialist nursing care to critically ill patients who require constant monitoring and advanced medical support. Working in a fast-paced hospital environment, ICU Nurses collaborate closely with doctors, respiratory therapists, and multidisciplinary teams to deliver life-saving treatment while supporting patients and their families.

Key Responsibilities

  • Monitor and assess critically ill patients around the clock.
  • Administer medications, intravenous therapies, and blood products safely.
  • Operate and monitor life-support equipment, including ventilators and cardiac monitors.
  • Recognise changes in patient conditions and respond quickly to emergencies.
  • Develop and implement individualised care plans.
  • Maintain accurate patient records and clinical documentation.
  • Work collaboratively with consultants, physicians, and allied healthcare professionals.
  • Support patients' families by providing updates, education, and emotional reassurance.
  • Adhere to infection prevention, safeguarding, and hospital safety protocols.
  • Participate in handovers, clinical audits, and ongoing professional development.

About You

The ideal candidate will have:

  • Registration as a qualified nurse with the appropriate regulatory body (such as the NMC for UK roles).
  • Experience in intensive care, critical care, emergency nursing, or a willingness to undertake ICU training.
  • Excellent clinical assessment and decision-making skills.
  • Strong communication and teamwork abilities.
  • Confidence working under pressure in high-acuity environments.
  • Compassion, resilience, and attention to detail.
  • Commitment to delivering safe, patient-centred care.
  • Current Basic Life Support (BLS) certification, with Advanced Life Support (ALS) being advantageous.

Benefits

  • Competitive salary of £36,000–£48,000 per year.
  • Full-time, stable employment with structured shifts.
  • Paid annual leave and statutory pension contributions.
  • Ongoing professional development and specialist ICU training opportunities.
  • Access to employee wellbeing and mental health support programmes.
  • Enhanced overtime and weekend pay where applicable.
  • Career progression into senior nursing, education, or leadership roles.
  • Supportive multidisciplinary working environment.
  • NHS or private healthcare benefits depending on the employer.
  • Uniform allowance or provided uniforms in many hospitals.
