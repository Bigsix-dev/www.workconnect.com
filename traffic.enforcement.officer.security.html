Traffic Enforcement Officer

Job Description

Local Authority manages local transport, parking, and public-space services to support safe and accessible communities. The authority is seeking a professional Traffic Enforcement Officer to monitor compliance with local traffic and parking regulations. You will patrol designated areas, identify violations, issue enforcement notices where appropriate, and provide information to members of the public while maintaining a fair and professional approach.

Responsibilities

  • Patrol designated roads, streets, car parks, and traffic-controlled areas.
  • Monitor compliance with parking and traffic restrictions.
  • Issue enforcement notices in accordance with relevant legislation and local procedures.
  • Record enforcement activities accurately using approved equipment and systems.
  • Respond professionally to questions and concerns from members of the public.
  • Report damaged signs, road markings, traffic equipment, and other hazards.
  • Identify potential traffic management issues and report them to the appropriate authority.
  • Work with colleagues and other public services to support safe and effective traffic management.

Requirements

  • No specific degree is required; previous enforcement, public service, or customer-facing experience is desirable.
  • Good ability to understand and follow traffic regulations and enforcement procedures.
  • Excellent communication and interpersonal skills.
  • Ability to deal confidently and professionally with members of the public.
  • Strong observation and attention to detail.
  • Good record-keeping and organisational skills.
  • Ability to work independently and follow established procedures.
  • Comfortable working outdoors in different weather conditions.

Working Hours

  • Full-time position.
  • 37 hours per week.
  • Working hours may include weekends and public holidays depending on operational requirements.

Salary

  • £26,000–£31,000 per year.

Benefits

  • Competitive local authority salary.
  • Workplace pension scheme.
  • Paid annual leave.
  • Traffic enforcement and professional training.
  • Uniform and required enforcement equipment.
  • Stable public-sector working environment.
  • Career development and progression opportunities.
  • Employee wellbeing and support programmes.
